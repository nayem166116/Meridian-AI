A practical guide to AI lead scoring for small sales teams

Tom Walsh, Head of Customer Success · 5 min read

If you're a five-to-fifteen person sales team, you don't need a data science team or a six-month scoring model rollout. You need a simple, explainable way to tell your reps which leads to call first. Here's a framework that works well for teams just getting started with AI-assisted lead scoring.

Start with three signal categories

Instead of dozens of weighted fields, group signals into three buckets:

  • Fit - does this person and company match your ideal customer profile? Title, company size, industry.
  • Intent - what have they actually done? Requested a demo, visited pricing twice, replied to an email.
  • Engagement freshness - how recently did they take that action? A demo request from today matters more than one from three weeks ago.

A lead that scores high on all three is your hottest lead. A lead that scores high on fit but has taken no action yet should go to a nurture sequence, not a rep's call list.

Let the agent explain itself

The biggest reason reps ignore a lead score is that they don't trust it. A raw number like "82" means nothing on its own. Good AI lead scoring shows its work: "Senior title with buying authority, company size matches ICP, requested a demo within the last 24 hours." When reps can see the reasoning, they act on the score instead of second-guessing it.

Review and recalibrate monthly

Pull a sample of scored leads each month and check them against what actually happened - did the hot leads convert? Did any cold leads turn out to be good? Feed that back into your scoring criteria. This doesn't require machine learning expertise, just a recurring 30-minute review with your sales lead.

A scoring system that reps trust and use consistently beats a more "accurate" model that sits unused.
